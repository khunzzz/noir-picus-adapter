## Q1. Known and fixed soundness bugs in Barretenberg (gadgets, memory, range/lookup, bigfield, ECDSA, recursion, Fiat–Shamir, sumcheck, predicates)

### Takeaway
Barretenberg has a long, documented history of soundness bugs. Many of them sit in exactly the layers the researcher's tool treats as opaque: logic (AND/XOR) gadgets, bigfield/non-native arithmetic, Pedersen, plookup tables used by ECDSA, recursive-verifier transcripts, and the IVC/Goblin sub-protocols. Findings were still arriving in 2026: a secp256r1 lookup-table soundness fix in July 2026, and two "critical" proving-system vulnerabilities publicly announced for Alpha v4 (found 2026-03-17) and Alpha v5 (found 2026-07-27). This layer is productive.

### Cited Findings
**Aztec's own bug catalogue (`barretenberg/security/entomaxy/List of security bugs.md`, last reorganised 2025-05-09 by Sarkoxed, [C/4d1a7c839](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/commit/4d1a7c839df7081da05c2086a6f312c8a405cc45))** — [T/security/entomaxy/List of security bugs.md](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/next/barretenberg/security/entomaxy/List%20of%20security%20bugs.md):
- BarVD-S-001 stdlib/BigField: "insufficient constraint for non-native field arithmetic", which allowed fraudulent recursive proofs. Found internally by manual review.
- BarVD-S-004 stdlib/BigField: "insufficient range checks while emulating non-native field operations", which allowed fraudulent recursive proofs. Found externally by Xin Gao and Onur Kilic ([hackmd disclosure](https://hackmd.io/@aztec-network/disclosure-of-recent-vulnerabilities)).
- BarVD-S-005 in-circuit Pedersen hash: "underconstrained 2-bit window representation of field elements", which allowed double spending. Found by @arielgabizon ([Medium](https://medium.com/aztec-protocol/vulnerabilities-found-in-aztec-2-0-9b80c8bf416c)).
- BarVD-S-006: the rollup circuit's point aggregation left out the join-split proof points, so fraudulent proofs could be proven recursively. This is an aggregation-object bug of the same kind that recursion blackboxes can have.
- BarVD-S-011 stdlib/logic: "3bit overflow in AND/XOR", impact "Malform AND/XOR/OR operations". It was **found by the tool `acir_formal_proofs`** (SMT) by @defkit and @Sarkoxed and fixed in PR [#11651](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/pull/11651). This is a bb-level under-constraint of the ACIR AND/XOR blackbox.
- BarVD-S-012 StandardCircuitBuilder logic gate: "Passing arbitrary values as XOR/AND results x2". It was **found by `smt_verification`** by @Sarkoxed and fixed in PR [#4530](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/pull/4530).
- BarVD-C-002 (completeness) cycle_group: adding two witnesses produced a constant. Found by @Sarkoxed ([PR #12385](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/pull/12385)).
- BarVD-C-003 bigfield overflow. Found by bigfield_fuzzer ([PR #9547](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/pull/9547)) and "further rediscovered by the auditors".
- BarVD-PG-001 Protogalaxy recursive verifier: the folded commitments C_c[i] were not hashed into the transcript, so they "could be updated after deriving the challenges"; impact "possible to recursively prove fraudulent proofs". Found by @Rumata888 using manual review plus the Origin Tag mechanism. Fixed in [PR #14004](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/pull/14004) (commit 2025-05-02, [C/6bf07d407](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/commit/6bf07d407f2d797152d9251c3c1654efd4240102)). ### Inferences
- Where bugs appear: most bb soundness bugs historically sit in (a) non-native/bigfield/biggroup arithmetic, which backs ECDSA, MSM and recursion; (b) lookup-table plumbing, where table IDs or indices collide; (c) logic/range decomposition; and (d) recursive verifiers and transcripts (Fiat–Shamir, aggregation objects, Goblin/Translator/ECCVM). All of these sit under blackbox opcodes the researcher's tool treats as deterministic and opaque.
- The 2025–2026 pace is high: bugs fixed through July 2026, two "critical" public disclosures in 2026, and large new code (ROM via logup in June 2026, secp256r1 fake-GLV in May 2026, optimized Poseidon2 in May/June 2026). New code is therefore still landing under the blackboxes.
- The Alpha v4 and v5 criticals are described as proving-system-wide (Chonk/IVC, PCS or the verifier). They may not be reachable from a standalone Noir UltraHonk program. The public descriptions do not say which component was affected.

## Q3. Does bb honor ACIR predicates, and where do ACVM and bb semantics diverge? Differential testing

### Takeaway
bb's DSL layer relies on several assumptions that "Noir already constrains X". For example, SHA-256 inputs are assumed to be 32-bit, and AES bytes were assumed range-checked until February 2026. Its handling of false predicates for ECDSA, MSM, EC-add and recursion replaces the inputs with dummy valid values and **leaves the output witnesses unconstrained**. That may be looser than ACVM's execution semantics. Aztec runs structural differential checks (`acir_components_check`, SSA-fuzzer → `bb prove/verify` workers, `acir_formal_proofs`). I found no public tool that checks semantic equivalence between ACVM and bb for all blackboxes.

### Cited Findings
- **Predicates**:
  - PR "use the bb predicates when generating constraints" (2025-10-13, [C/4d916dd3c](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/commit/4d916dd3cfd6f2380e562c073512347eec1a1de3)). "For recursive proving, we conditionally assign the inputs when the predicate is false to a placeholder verifiable proof and vk"; "For elliptic curve points, we conditionally assign the inputs when the predicate is false, to valid dummy values"; "ecdsa r1 is not done on purpose". This implies that before October 2025 bb ignored predicates for these opcodes.
  - ECDSA: when the predicate is witness-false, bb sets r = s = H(m) = 1 and pk = 2·G. The final step is `signature_result.assert_equal(bool_ct::conditional_assign(predicate, result, signature_result))`, so with predicate = 0 the ACIR `result` witness is **not tied to anything** — [T/cpp/src/barretenberg/dsl/acir_format/ecdsa_constraints.cpp](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/next/barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/dsl/acir_format/ecdsa_constraints.cpp).
  - MSM: `to_be_asserted_equal = conditional_assign(input.predicate, input.result, result); result.assert_equal(...)`. The docstring says "In the case of predicate equal to witness false, the constraint is supposed to be inactive, so even if input_result is not checked to be on the curve in this case, it is OK" — [T/…/multi_scalar_mul.cpp](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/next/barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/dsl/acir_format/multi_scalar_mul.cpp).
  - Honk recursion: a witness-false predicate conditionally assigns a valid vk, proof and vk hash, and the comments say the new witnesses "don't create a soundness issue" — [T/…/honk_recursion_constraint.cpp](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/next/barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/dsl/acir_format/honk_recursion_constraint.cpp).
  - There is a dedicated predicate test harness, `test_class_predicate.hpp` — [T/…/dsl/acir_format/](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/tree/next/barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/dsl/acir_format).
- **Input-range assumptions in blackbox gadgets**:
  - SHA-256 compression: "It is assumed that the caller (Noir) separately constrains all 24 inputs (8 hash state + 16 message words) to 32 bits" — [T/…/sha256_constraint.cpp](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/next/barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/dsl/acir_format/sha256_constraint.cpp). A defensive constraint on `w_in[0]` was added on 2026-05-15 ([C/5d43e115a](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/commit/5d43e115a2212efd0b9062285ec4f8b22f57aac7)).
  - AES: "Noir enforces bytes to be in the range [0, 255] by type declarations, however, if inputs are taken from ACIR directly, these ranges should be enforced". Byte range checks were added in February 2026 ([C/4e1231c93](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/commit/4e1231c93927467e8125918501e515d352ad0503)).
  - Blake2s: the `byte_array_ct` constructor adds per-byte range constraints — [T/…/blake2s_constraint.cpp](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/next/barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/dsl/acir_format/blake2s_constraint.cpp).
- **AND/XOR**: stdlib logic splits operands into 32-bit chunks with explicit range checks on a short final chunk and "checks [the reconstruction] against the original value". The docstring says operands larger than `num_bits` are "truncated", while the native prover asserts msb < num_bits — [T/cpp/src/barretenberg/stdlib/primitives/logic/logic.cpp](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/next/barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/stdlib/primitives/logic/logic.cpp). The 3-bit overflow in this gadget (BarVD-S-011) was a real soundness bug ([catalogue](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/next/barretenberg/security/entomaxy/List%20of%20security%20bugs.md)).
- **MSM scalars ≥ Grumpkin modulus**: tests added on 2026-05-14 show that r, r+1 and 2^254−1 make the circuit unsatisfiable, and that `5+r` does not re-prove the output of `5` ([C/4be7c4c8b](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/commit/4be7c4c8b698a403874e5f07e6949d76d5a22d4a)). Point-at-infinity tests for EC add and MSM were added on 2026-05-04 ([C/dfdd7fca7](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/commit/dfdd7fca717e79805751077bb1339d8d23952acf)).
- **MemoryOp**:
  - Since 2026-05-12, `MemOp.index` and `MemOp.value` must be single unscaled witnesses. The deserializer asserts this and drops the constant-ops path "since the path being exercised never came up in production" ([C/ef61c7ca3](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/commit/ef61c7ca35b99c4c6433c452b171d3656973327c)).
  - The DSL README says bb asserts that the op-type expression is a constant 0 or 1 — [T/…/dsl/acir_format/README.md](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/next/barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/dsl/acir_format/README.md).
  - ROM out-of-bounds reads are caught by `BB_ASSERT_LT(native_index, length)` on the prover side — [T/…/stdlib/primitives/memory/rom_table.cpp](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/next/barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/stdlib/primitives/memory/rom_table.cpp).
  - ROM was re-implemented as a logUp relation on 2026-06-19 (`(index, value)` in w1/w2, `array_id` in the q_c selector, multiplicity in w3, inverse in w4) — [C/d3e42f9d3](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/commit/d3e42f9d3a31ee0ccd8a36055a13f69508d3a65f).

- **Differential and structural tooling**:
  - **`acir_components_check`** (defkit, 2026-03-24, [C/bb900076b](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/commit/bb900076bf43a72adc9ab6438eefea4555d63b60)) loads ACIR bytecode, runs `create_circuit` into an `UltraCircuitBuilder`, and checks that every ACIR connected component (witnesses co-occurring in an opcode) maps to one bb circuit component. ### Inferences
- **Concrete lead for the researcher (unverified, needs a test).** Under a witness-false predicate, bb leaves the ECDSA `result` witness and the MSM and EC-add output witnesses free. If ACVM assigns a fixed value, such as 0, in that case, then bb admits more witnesses than ACVM. Any Noir codegen that uses a predicated blackbox output without masking it by the predicate would then carry a prover-chosen value. The adapter should model predicated blackbox outputs as **nondeterministic when predicate = 0**, not as deterministic functions of the inputs, and check whether Noir's ACIR-gen always multiplies or masks such outputs. I did not verify ACVM's false-predicate output convention in this session.
- **Second lead.** The bb gadgets for SHA-256 (and AES before February 2026) trust ACIR-level range constraints on their inputs. If a Noir codegen path, such as unchecked casts, `as u32` on a Field, or unconstrained-to-constrained boundaries, emits `Sha256Compression` without 32-bit RANGE on every input word, the bb gadget's behaviour on out-of-range inputs is unspecified. It may be non-unique, since the lookup decomposition may accept several encodings. The tool could flag every blackbox input that lacks a dominating RANGE at the ACIR level. This is a cheap static pass.
- `acir_components_check` is a structural (graph-connectivity) check, not a uniqueness check. It would miss under-constraint *within* a connected component, which is exactly the class the logic 3-bit overflow and the secp256r1 lookup-index collision belonged to.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm ACVM's semantics for blackbox outputs under a false predicate, or whether Noir SSA/ACIR-gen masks them. This needs a check of `acvm-repo/acvm/src/pwg/blackbox/*` in noir-lang/noir.
- I did not check RANGE with num_bits ≥ 254 or with very large widths in bb (`range_constraint.hpp` only defines the struct), or bb's handling of `MemoryInit` with `Calldata`/`Returndata` in a standalone UltraCircuitBuilder beyond the error message in `block_constraint.cpp`.
- I found no public paper on differential testing of ACVM against bb constraints. The Diligence/TU Wien work fuzzes the "Noir zero-knowledge pipeline" (snippet), but I could not read its methodology.

## Q4. Tools to extract and check the actual bb circuit (smt_verification, acir_formal_proofs, circuit checker)

### Takeaway
`barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/smt_verification` is a maintained (commits through 2026-05-28) cvc5-based framework, mainly by Sarkoxed (with defkit/jewelofchaos9 for `acir_formal_proofs`). It exports bb Ultra circuits (gates, lookup tables, ROM/RAM) and supports **two-copy uniqueness queries** (`UltraCircuit::unique_witness_ext`), the same self-composition idea as Picus, directly on the bb gate level. It found at least two real soundness bugs in bb's AND/XOR (BarVD-S-011, S-012). This is the most direct way to push the researcher's method one layer down.

### Cited Findings
- **Workflow**: `builder.set_variable_name(...)` → `export_circuit()` (msgpack) → `smt_circuit::unpack_from_file` → `smt_solver::Solver` (cvc5, finite-field solver options `ff_solver` "gb"/"split-gb", `ff_elim_disjunctive_bit`, `lookup_enabled` for set-theory lookups) → `UltraCircuit(schema, &solver, TermType)` with term types FFTerm, FFITerm, ITerm and BVTerm (bitvector, for uint/bitwise) — [T/cpp/src/barretenberg/smt_verification/README.md](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/next/barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/smt_verification/README.md).
- **Uniqueness API**: `UltraCircuit::unique_witness_ext(circuit_info, solver, type, equal_variables, nequal_variables, at_least_one_equal, at_least_one_nequal)` "create[s] two separate circuits, constrain[s] variables with names from `equal` to be equal across the circuits, and set[s] all the other variables to be not equal". `smt_util` can write back the solver model as a witness and re-check it with `CircuitChecker::check` — [README](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/next/barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/smt_verification/README.md).
- **Symbolic data structures and memory support**: "Data Structures" (SymArray, SymSet, STuple; PR #13658, 2025-04-24) and "RAM/ROM tables handler in SMT verification module" (PR #14150, 2025-05-12). ### Inferences
- **Recommended method (a direct lift of the researcher's pipeline one layer down)**:
  1. For each ACIR blackbox and memory opcode, build a minimal ACIR program: inputs as public or fixed witnesses, outputs as named witnesses, and the ACIR-level RANGE constraints exactly as Noir emits them.
  2. Build it with bb `create_circuit` and name the variables mapped from ACIR witness indices through `real_variable_index`.
  3. Export with `export_circuit()`.
  4. Run `unique_witness_ext` with the ACIR inputs and predicate equal and the outputs not equal.

  UNSAT means the bb gadget determines the output uniquely given the ACIR-level input assumptions. SAT is either a real gadget bug (like BarVD-S-011) or an unstated input-range assumption; both are reportable. Expected scaling: AND/XOR/RANGE, Poseidon2 and memory are feasible (the bitwise ops were solved in seconds to minutes in `acir_formal_proofs`). SHA-256, Blake, Keccak, bigfield ECDSA and recursion will likely time out as whole circuits. Those need slicing to sub-gadgets, such as a single lookup-table decomposition, a single bigfield mul/reduce, or a single NAF step.
- **Cheaper, high-yield complements**: run `acir_components_check` over the researcher's large corpus of compiled Noir programs, since it is already a binary. Add a mutation mode: flip a single gate or selector in the exported bb circuit and check whether the ACIR-level test still fails (this tests test-suite adequacy). Also differentially check ACVM witness solving against `CircuitChecker` on randomised and edge-case blackbox inputs: point at infinity, scalars ≥ r, bytes > 255, u32 words ≥ 2^32, predicate = 0 with garbage inputs, ROM index = len.
- Priority targets, based on recency of rewrites and the history above: (1) the secp256r1 fixed-base + fake-GLV ECDSA path (May 2026, already had one soundness bug); (2) ROM via logUp (June 2026); (3) optimized Poseidon2 / "−2 rows/perm" (May–June 2026, branch named `poseidon2-bridge-row-soundness`, [PR #189](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/pull/189)); (4) predicate-false paths of ECDSA, MSM, EC-add and recursion; (5) bigfield/biggroup NAF and edge cases, which have a repeated bug history.

### Gaps
- I did not find whether smt_verification currently runs in Aztec CI. The CI integration commit (September 2025) was reverted and re-applied, and its current state is unknown.
- I found no public paper or talk on smt_verification's methodology or a full list of its findings beyond the two catalogue entries. I could not determine whether it handles the new logUp ROM relation or the optimized Poseidon2 gates. The 2026-05 trace-layout re-alignment commits suggest it tracks builder changes, but I did not confirm this.

## Q5. Formal verification of bb gadgets or ACIR semantics

### Takeaway
Formal work on bb itself is internal and SMT-based: `smt_verification` for gadget uniqueness and equivalence, and `acir_formal_proofs` for SSA-op → ACIR → bb-circuit correctness on integer/bitwise ops. It is incomplete: division, mod and field truncation time out, and blackbox hashes, ECDSA and recursion are not covered. I found no public mechanised (Lean/Coq) proof of bb gadgets or of ACIR→bb translation.

### Cited Findings
- `acir_formal_proofs` covers arithmetic, bitwise, shifts, comparisons, casts, Not and Truncate. Div, Mod and Field→u64 Truncate are unresolved (TIMEOUT) as of 2026-03-20 — [README](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/next/barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/acir_formal_proofs/README.md).
- smt_verification includes polynomial-evaluation and function-equality examples (`smt_polynomials.test.cpp`, `smt_examples.test.cpp`) and a `subcircuits.test.cpp` — [T/cpp/src/barretenberg/smt_verification/](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/tree/next/barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/smt_verification).
- zkSecurity used SageMath symbolic simplification of bigfield custom-gate expressions during their audit (snippet) — [zkSecurity report](https://zksecurity.xyz/reports/aztec-bigfield).
- Consensys Diligence and TU Wien published ZK-infrastructure fuzzing research (ACM CCS; a zkVM soundness/completeness testing paper at USENIX Security 2026) and received an Aztec award for "detecting soundness and completeness bugs in the Noir zero-knowledge pipeline" (snippets) — [Diligence blog](https://diligence.security/blog/2026/03/fresh-paint-deep-roots./); [TU Wien TISS](https://tiss.tuwien.ac.at/api/pdb/rest/project/link/Aztec%20Award%20(joint%20with%20Diligence%20Security)%20for%20detecting%20soundness%20and%20completeness%20bugs%20in%20the%20Noir%20zero-knowledge%20pipeline/2844414).

### Inferences
- There is a clear opening that no public work fills: uniqueness proofs or bug findings for bb's **blackbox** gadgets under exact ACIR-level preconditions, and a written spec of the ACVM ↔ bb contract (predicates, input ranges, constant inputs). The researcher's Picus self-composition expertise transfers directly, through `smt_verification`'s `unique_witness_ext` on exported bb circuits.
- The ACIR-level blind spot is real and has been exploited before. Two catalogued bb soundness bugs (BarVD-S-011 and S-012) plus the 2026 secp256r1 lookup-index fix were all under blackbox opcodes an ACIR-only tool models as sound deterministic functions.

### Gaps
- I did not check whether Noir or Aztec published a formal ACIR semantics document (for example, predicate semantics per blackbox) in 2026.
- I did not find Lean or Coq efforts on Barretenberg; this is a negative result from limited search, not proof of absence.
